%  Abstract

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present \modnet{}, a self-growing modular recurrent network whose
topology is discovered through evolution rather than imposed by a
designer. The architecture has no predefined layers: each node
computes a weighted sum of its inputs followed by a threshold
activation, and any node may connect to any other node, including
itself. Starting from a minimal random topology, the network grows,
prunes, and reorganizes itself through a compact evolutionary loop to
solve tasks ranging from Boolean logic (XOR, parity of four and six
bits) to continuous regression (sinusoids, quadratic surfaces). We
report exact solutions ($100\%$ accuracy) on XOR and 4-bit parity, and
root-mean-square errors below $0.05$ on continuous targets, using
fewer than $30$ nodes per network. Across all tasks we observe that
cross-module connectivity, recurrence, and sparse wiring emerge
spontaneously as the task demands, without any explicit regularization
or architectural prior. Our results suggest that layer-free
computation driven by a minimal evolutionary process can rediscover
structural principles typically imposed by human designers.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} neuroevolution, self-growing networks, layer-free
architectures, modular recurrent networks, Boolean function learning,
sparse connectivity.
\end{abstract}
